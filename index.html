📜 Welcome to Doctrine & Covenants Geography

Welcome to the Interactive Historical Atlas of the Doctrine & Covenants (~1805 – 1890), built to accompany its sister scriptural atlases of the Old Testament, New Testament, and Book of Mormon.

Key Capabilities:

  • Strictly Chronological Sequence: While canonized D&C sections are not in chronological order (e.g. Section 1 was received in November 1831 in Hiram, OH, while Section 2 was received in September 1823 in Manchester, NY), this atlas places every revelation and historical milestone in exact historical chronological order.
  • Dual Archival Links: Every site and revelation connects directly to authentic scripture text on ChurchofJesusChrist.org and original historical manuscript scans on JosephSmithPapers.org.
  • 100+ Authentic Historic Locations: Explore sacred sites, printing shops, homes, and temple sites across Vermont, New York, Pennsylvania, Ohio, Missouri, Illinois, Iowa, Nebraska, Wyoming, and Utah.
  • Historical Expeditions: Follow exact routes for Zion's Camp (1834) and the Mormon Pioneer Trail (1846–1847).

🕯️ Frontier Life in the 19th Century

Printing & Publishing

In 1829, Egbert B. Grandin operated a Smith-patented hand press in Palmyra, requiring printer's devils to hand-ink type with leather ink-balls and press sheets one by one. The first 5,000 copies of the Book of Mormon required 3 million impressions.

Overland Travel & River Flatboats

Before transcontinental railways, traveling from New York to Missouri required canal boats on the Erie Canal, steamboats on the Ohio and Missouri rivers, or walking hundreds of miles through mud and malaria-ridden swamps.

The Sacred Gathering

Latter-day Saints believed literally in the physical gathering of the righteous out of Babylon into Zion—moving successively from New York to Kirtland, Ohio, Jackson County and Far West, Missouri, Nauvoo, Illinois, and across the plains to the Salt Lake Valley.
